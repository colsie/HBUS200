Course Description

Welcome to Future of Leadership!

As part of this unique, interdisciplinary course, you'll delve into how leaders make decisions in plural societies.

In this course, you'll embark on a Leader's Journey, discovering your unique leadership style and building critical skills that will prepare you to navigate and lead in uncertain times. We will explore foundational leadership theories, develop problem-solving abilities, and learn how to build effective teams. As you progress through the course, you'll map out strategic visions, influence others, and make decisions that drive impactful outcomes.

You will apply the theories in this course to a real-life challenge in the form of your "squad" project, and the frameworks we learn here will prepare you for the . Whether you aspire to lead in business, engineering, technology, healthcare, education, the arts, government, or any other field, the problem-solving skills and knowledge you acquire here will be invaluable in your future career. By the end of the semester, you'll be well-equipped to lead with confidence and resilience in any field.

Let's begin this exciting journey together and prepare to become the leaders of tomorrow!

This course is designed for exceptional students enrolled in the Interdisciplinary Business Honors program, who have already completed HBUS100 and HBUS105.

By the end, you'll be able to
  • 01
    Develop and apply a structured problem-solving process that integrates multiple perspectives to address complex leadership challenges.
  • 02
    Demonstrate understanding of socially-driven constructions of difference — cultural, historical, social, ideological — and their impact on leadership practice.
  • 03
    Use a comparative framework to examine how cultural, historical, or social context shapes leadership strategy across organizations and societies.

Course policies

Rules for the Journey

Tap a policy to expand it.

One missed class with advance email notice is acceptable per university policy. Absences longer than one session, or more than twice in the semester, require documentation. Arriving late or leaving early by more than 15 minutes counts as an absence. Continued undocumented absences will affect your participation grade.

Late assignments need prior instructor permission and a legitimate reason. You get one 48-hour grace period per term on an eligible individual assignment if requested before the due date. Beyond that, late work must land within 48 hours for a −50% penalty; after 48 hours it isn't accepted.

Questions and instructor assistance are only available before the posted deadline — the grace period is for finishing the work, not for extra help.

All team assignments are due on time since they're presented in class — email the instructor immediately if this is at risk. An unexcused absence during a team presentation earns no credit for that assignment for the absent member.

Major Scheduled Graded Events — Exercise #1, Exercise #2, and the final Leader's Journey paper — only accept University-approved absences (religious observance, documented emergencies, documented illness).

Phones, laptops, headphones, and other devices are off-limits during class unless a specific exercise calls for them. Full attention keeps discussion productive — violations can affect your participation grade.

Cite AI use like any other source. You may use AI for brainstorming, drafting, refining, and editing outside-of-class work, as long as the use is disclosed at the bottom of the assignment.

Some in-class assignments will require you to demonstrate your understanding independently.

Reach out within one week of receiving a grade if you have a concern. The IBH Leadership team (Faculty Director, Associate Director, Program Coordinator) is glad to hear course feedback, but the instructional team is your first stop for resolving grading issues.

Please familiarize yourself with UMD's Code of Academic Integrity. Course-assistance sites (e.g. CourseHero) and AI-generated content aren't permitted sources unless explicitly allowed for that assignment. You may discuss course concepts in open forums, but collaboration on graded work is prohibited unless stated otherwise.

On every exam or assignment, you sign: "I pledge on my honor that I have not given or received any unauthorized assistance on this exam/assignment." If you're ever unsure what's acceptable, ask first.
